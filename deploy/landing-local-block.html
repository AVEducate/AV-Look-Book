Local first

Your show stays yours.

AV Look Book works on your computer, with your files. Nothing about your show goes anywhere unless you export it or send it.

  • Your project files stay on your computer. A show is a single .avlb file you control.
  • Works offline. No Internet needed to build the show, no dependence on ballroom Wi-Fi, fine on isolated production networks. When online, the app only checks for a newer version at launch.
  • No cloud workspace, no account. Projects are not tied to a login and are not stored in an online workspace.
  • No analytics, no AI. The app collects no usage data and sends no project information through AI systems.
  • Back it up your way. Copy the file to the show drive, a USB stick, the company server or your approved cloud folder, like the rest of the show.

Companies keep their own software and IT rules, of course. Local, offline operation just means there is a lot less to review.

Handoff

Turn show knowledge into company knowledge.

"John knows how that room works." "Ask Sarah what P08 does." "The engineer who built it isn't here today." Corporate AV runs on freelancers, rotating crews and last-minute substitutions, and the show shouldn't disappear when the engineer goes home.

With the project file, the Day 2 operator, the replacement Show Caller, the Technical Director reviewing the show or the Producer checking the screen layouts all open the same show. No user provisioning, no seats, no passwords, no freelancer account setup, nobody to remove after the show.

Send the show. Not another login.

Recurring client? Open last year's file, Save As, change what is different. Don't rebuild recurring shows from memory.

Fits your workflow

Use it with the workflow you already have.

Build it in AV Look Book, share it in the format your crew needs. The PDF look book and the Excel cue sheet go to everyone; the project file goes to anyone with the app. The whole crew does not have to adopt another platform to read the show, and the documentation stays usable outside the app.

AV Look Book plans and documents the show. It does not control the switcher, and it is not a replacement for Vectorworks, ConnectCAD or AutoCAD: the integrator documents how the system is built, AV Look Book documents how it is being used for this event. If you already speak sources, destinations, presets, AUX and signal flow, you already speak its language.
