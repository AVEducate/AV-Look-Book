What it does

Built by a show caller. Speaks the room's language.

Destinations, presets, layers, DSM and AUX, I/O and cabling. Nothing to learn that you don't already say on comms.

Video Presets

One look per preset, on the real canvas.

Add your destinations at their real resolutions, drop sources onto layers, set DSM and AUX outputs, and capture each look as P01, P02, P03, exactly the way you'll call it.

Every preset page in the look book comes straight from this canvas.

Wire

Signal flow that prints true.

Sources into the switcher, switcher out to every destination and AUX, with cable colours by connector type. HDMI in and SDI out looks like HDMI in and SDI out.

Simple gives you the one-page sheet. Advanced gives you routers, port numbers, primary and backup runs, and multi-page drawings.

I/O Patch

The inventory the engineers ask for.

Connector, type, resolution, slot and notes for every source, destination and AUX. Edit it once and it's reflected on the wire diagram, the router tiles and the exports.

What you hand the crew
Look Book PDF

Cover, I/O reference, one page per preset with a screen-by-screen breakdown, the wire sheet, and a summary table.

Excel cue sheet

Every preset and destination as a spreadsheet for the switcher op and the stage manager.

Send in one click

PDF, Excel and the show file attached to a new email, with a link so the recipient can open the show themselves.

First launch on a Mac

AV Look Book isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS stops the very first open. One time only: click Done, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and click Open Anyway.

If it says the app is damaged, paste this in Terminal and open it again: xattr -cr "/Applications/AV Look Book.app"

First launch on Windows

SmartScreen will say the publisher is unknown. Click More info, then Run anyway. One time only.

Your shows live in Documents › AV Look Book, one .avlb file per show, saved automatically. Questions: Info@aveducate.com.
