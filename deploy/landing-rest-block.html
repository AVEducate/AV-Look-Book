04/Solve it before show site

The switcher shouldn't be where you figure out the show.

Building the project makes you answer the questions before load-in. What are the sources? What are the destinations, and at what resolution? What goes to DSM, what goes to AUX? What does each preset do? How is it all connected? Planning the show becomes part of documenting the show.

The things that usually surface in rehearsal surface on your laptop instead:

  • Missing sources or destinations
  • Wrong resolutions
  • Routing problems and missing outputs
  • AUX or DSM conflicts
  • Connector mistakes
  • Looks that don't fit the destination
  • Inconsistent preset naming
  • Unclear responsibilities between departments
Faster rehearsals

Instead of "change that look with the camera on the left and slides on the right", the team says "change P07". Everyone is looking at the same picture.

Approve it before show day

Producers and clients see the audience screens, IMAG placement, walk-in and playback looks on the real screen shapes, so "that's not what I thought it would look like" happens in pre-production.

Preflight, no extra checklist

You don't fill in a form. You build the show, and the questions get answered on the way.

05/How it works

Built by a show caller. Speaks the room's language.

Destinations, presets, layers, DSM and AUX, I/O, routing and cabling. Nothing to learn that you don't already say on comms, and one project behind every document.

Video Presets

One look per preset, on the real canvas

Add your destinations at their real resolutions, drop sources onto layers, set DSM and AUX outputs, and capture each look as P01, P02, P03, exactly the way you'll call it.

Every preset page in the look book comes straight from this canvas.

Wire

Signal flow that prints true

Sources into the switcher, switcher out to every destination and AUX, with cable colours by connector type. HDMI in and SDI out looks like HDMI in and SDI out.

Simple gives you the one-page sheet. Advanced gives you routers, switchers, converters and network switches with port numbers, primary and backup runs, a cable type per output, and multi-page drawings that print from Letter up to ANSI E.

I/O Patch

The inventory the engineers ask for

Connector, type, resolution, slot and notes for every source, destination and AUX. Edit it once and it's reflected on the wire diagram, the router tiles and the exports.

09/Local first

Your show stays yours.

AV Look Book works on your computer, with your files. Nothing about your show goes anywhere unless you export it or send it.

  • Your project files stay on your computer. A show is a single .avlb file you control.
  • Works offline. No Internet needed to build the show, no dependence on ballroom Wi-Fi, fine on isolated production networks. When online, the app only checks for a newer version at launch.

13/Questions before you download

Straight answers.

Is it really free?

Yes. No trial, no subscription, no seats, no account. AV Educate Inc is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit; AV Look Book is built as a resource for the AV community and funded by donations.

Why do you ask for my email?

It does not create an AV Look Book account, and your projects are not tied to it. AV Educate uses the email for AV Look Book updates and news. Unsubscribe any time.

Does it need the Internet?

No. AV Look Book works fully offline. When it is online, the app checks GitHub for a newer version at launch. Nothing about your show is sent.

Where are my files stored?

On your computer. A show is a single .avlb file that you control, plus an autosave draft in the app's own data folder. Back it up like any show file.

Does AV Educate see my project? Does it upload anything?

No. There is no cloud workspace, no analytics, no telemetry and no AI. The only network traffic is the update check at launch and links you click yourself.

Does it control my switcher?

No. AV Look Book plans and documents the show. Programming stays on the switcher.

Does it replace Vectorworks, ConnectCAD or AutoCAD?

No. Those document how the system is built. AV Look Book documents how the system is being used for this event.

Does it work with my switcher?

It is brand-agnostic. Plan the show whether it ends up on Barco, Analog Way, Pixelhue, Spyder, Ross or something else. The Vocabulary reference in the app maps the terms between them.

Does the whole crew need AV Look Book?

No. Build it in the app, then hand out the PDF look book, the Excel cue sheet or the project file. Anyone with the app opens the file and gets the whole show.

Can I send the project to another engineer?

Yes. Send attaches the PDF, the Excel and the show file to a new email. The file opens on any Mac or Windows machine with the app.

Can I reuse an old show?

Yes. Open last year's file, Save As, change what is different. Or start from one of the example shows in Quick Start.

How do I back up my project?

It is a file. Copy it to the show drive, a USB stick, the company file server or your approved cloud folder, like the rest of the show.

Mac and Windows?

Mac (Apple Silicon and Intel) and Windows 10 / 11. Updates arrive inside the app. The Windows installer installs for the current user and needs no administrator rights.

Why does my computer warn me on first open?

The builds are not signed yet. Apple notarization and a Windows certificate cost money, and donations get us there. Mac: System Settings, Privacy & Security, Open Anyway. Windows: More info, Run anyway. The welcome email has the same steps.

What happens if AV Educate stops developing it?

Your files stay yours and stay readable, and the PDF and Excel exports keep the show usable outside the app.
